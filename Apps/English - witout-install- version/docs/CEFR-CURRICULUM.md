# CEFR grammar curriculum audit

## Method

The CEFR defines proficiency outcomes and grammatical control, but it is
deliberately non-prescriptive: it does not publish one mandatory English
grammar-topic list. The curriculum therefore uses:

1. The Council of Europe CEFR framework and grammatical-accuracy descriptors
   for A1–C2 proficiency.
2. Cambridge English Profile as the English-specific research framework that
   relates vocabulary and grammar to CEFR levels.
3. British Council and Test-English inventories as concrete lesson-topic
   checks.

Primary references:

- [Council of Europe: the CEFR is non-prescriptive](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/introduction-and-context)
- [Council of Europe: English-specific Reference Level Descriptions](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/reference-level-descriptions)
- [Council of Europe CEFR descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search)
- [Cambridge English: CEFR and English Profile](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/cefr/)
- [British Council A1–A2 grammar](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2)
- [British Council B1–B2 grammar](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2)
- [British Council C1 grammar](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/c1)
- [Test-English complete level inventory](https://test-english.com/grammar-points/contents/)

## Result

The 84 legacy units remain the unchanged core. The audit added 28 explicit
subjects that were absent or only implicit:

### A1

- Possessive ’s and whose
- Prepositions of time: at, in, on

### A2

- Adjectives and prepositions
- Adjectives ending in -ed and -ing
- Infinitive of purpose
- Quantifiers: few, a few, little, a little

### B1

- Capital letters and apostrophes
- Contrasting ideas: although, despite, however
- Used to, be used to, get used to
- Intensifiers: so and such
- Past ability: could, was able to, managed to
- Past habits: used to, would, past simple
- Phrasal verbs
- Present perfect: just, yet, still, already
- Reflexive pronouns
- Stative and dynamic verbs
- Using as and like
- Verbs and prepositions

### B2

- Gradable and non-gradable adjectives
- British and American grammar
- Modifying comparatives
- Reported speech: questions
- Future degrees of certainty
- Gerund or infinitive: change in meaning

### C1

- Advanced passives review
- Modals and expressions of probability
- Unreal time
- Word order in phrasal verbs

### C2

The CEFR C2 descriptor requires consistent control of complex grammar even
while attention is occupied by planning and monitoring. It does not prescribe
a separate finite topic list. The existing 14 C2 units therefore remain
competence-focused: marked word order, layered subordination, nuanced
modality, counterfactual distance, cohesion, rhetorical and academic
compression, idiomatic patterns, ambiguity control, style shifting,
punctuation, naturalness editing, genre grammar, and integrated automatic
production.

## Exercise contract

All 112 units expose at least five controlled exercises. Supplemental units
use one error-repair item plus four cloze items. Legacy units retain their
three original items and add rule retrieval and transfer retrieval. Grammar
Lab then adds up to three original-production prompts, so a normal lesson
contains eight practice steps.

Automated tests enforce:

- 84 unchanged legacy units;
- 112 total units;
- all six levels A1–C2;
- at least five controlled exercises for every unit;
- both explanation and exercise resource roles for every unit.

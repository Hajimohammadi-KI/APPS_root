# English Automaticity — daily learning methodology

## Product goal

The app does not treat completion, a streak, or recognition as fluency. Each
day converts one CEFR-aligned lesson into accurate, retrievable, spoken and
transferable language. The learner sees a small daily surface, but the lesson
content is not shortened or removed.

## One lesson, one evidence chain

Grammar Lab is the source of truth. Selecting **Use today** sends the exact
lesson title, CEFR level, rule, examples, common error, controlled exercises,
model answer and resource links into the single **Today’s Practice** route.
Daily training and automaticity evidence are two stages of that same route,
not separate destinations. Each lesson has its own saved keys so evidence from
one grammar topic cannot complete another topic.

The visible mission uses three manageable cards:

1. **Activate and use accurately** — retrieve the rule and complete controlled
   items from the selected lesson.
2. **Automate and write** — produce personal sentences, receive correction,
   inspect the reason and save the corrected form.
3. **Speak freely and transfer** — listen, shadow, record or dictate, then use
   the same pattern without reading in a new situation.

Behind those cards, mastery keeps separate evidence for recognition, recall,
supported use, independent use, transfer and delayed review. A card may close
for today while the topic remains `learning` or `usable`; only retained,
accurate evidence can advance it to `stable` or `automatic`.

## Grammar, vocabulary and speaking automaticity

- Grammar always comes from the chosen lesson and is checked in original
  output, not only multiple choice.
- Vocabulary comes from the lesson examples, topic prompts and Integrated
  Skills unit. The learner reuses a small relevant set in writing and speech;
  it is reviewed after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days.
- Speaking moves from model listening to sentence shadowing, complete-passage
  shadowing and a 45–60 second unscripted transfer. Typed transcript remains an
  accessibility fallback, but a level is not presented as speaking-verified
  without speaking evidence.
- Errors create a repair item and delayed review instead of only reducing a
  score.

## CEFR adaptation

- **A1–A2:** short concrete situations, one pattern at a time, slower read
  aloud work and supported personal sentences.
- **B1–B2:** connected answers, reasons, contrasts, collocations and transfer
  into work, health, travel and study situations.
- **C1–C2:** precise stance, register, nuance, synthesis, reformulation and
  evidence-based academic or professional output.

CEFR describes performance. The app therefore reports internal practice and
evidence status honestly and does not claim an external CEFR certificate.

## Motivation without reducing quality

- The learner receives one clear next action and progress at 33, 66 and 100
  percent.
- A completed card closes, while its saved evidence remains reviewable.
- Success messages describe the evidence created, not generic praise.
- Missing a day, illness or rest never destroys the learning chain. A short
  rescue session returns the learner to the next useful action.
- The daily time can be 10–60 minutes. Shorter time changes the amount for that
  day, not the level standard or mastery gate.
- Streaks support routine but cannot replace writing, speaking, transfer and
  delayed recall.

## Accessibility contract

The default experience uses high contrast, large targets, low visual noise,
plain instructions, one primary action at a time, read-aloud support and
Persian explanations on hover/focus. Work is untimed unless timing itself is
the learning objective. Instructions are broken into short steps for an older
learner with attention, reading or writing difficulties, while the complete
lesson and advanced options remain available.

## Persistence and privacy

Progress, settings, answers, assessment evidence and review queues are stored
locally; recordings use IndexedDB when enabled. Export/import and a selected
backup folder protect the learner from data loss. Neon remains optional until
identity, authorization, consent, conflict resolution and audio-retention
rules are approved.
